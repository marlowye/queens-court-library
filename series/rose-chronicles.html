THE LIES THAT HOLD US TOGETHER

The Secrets

Prologue

Before the lies, there was a small town nestled in the south of Scotland, known as Ashbourne. A town that, no matter how hard you searched the maps, you would never find. You would probably assume it had been invented by someone who had never set foot there.

But Ashbourne existed. And it still does. Following a territorial dispute, the town was divided in two. Two pieces of a puzzle whose story is far from over.

"Some things are never truly buried. They simply learn to wear another face."

History & Background

Before the division, Ashbourne was just another town in the south of Scotland, whose economy depended on a local industry controlled by influential families. For generations, these families had accumulated land, property, and political power. The town grew around this structure, and a great number of its inhabitants depended, directly or indirectly, on that industry to survive.

Until the company fell into crisis and shut its doors.

Hundreds of people lost their jobs. Small businesses disappeared, families lost their homes, and the inequality that had once been concealed beneath an illusion of stability became impossible to ignore.

That was when the dispute began.

The traditional families wanted to preserve their properties and influence. Others advocated for land redistribution, new investments, and a different administration. The disagreement escalated into a territorial and political dispute. After months of conflict, Ashbourne was officially divided into two districts.

But the division solved nothing.

The Rose Chronicles was the first district to be established. At the forefront stood the Ashford and Kensington families, promising to remain devoted to tradition and refusing to surrender everything they had built simply to satisfy people who thought more with their stomachs than with their minds.

Their community grew, bringing with it everything the Bible preached — or almost everything. They built family ties that, let's be honest, became rather intertwined.

The division did not make the poor of Rose Chronicles any richer, but it certainly enriched those whose bank accounts were already overflowing with millions.

It enriched perversity, lust, and arrogance.

It raised children on a foundation of lies, and families who simply wanted to live ordinary lives learned to remain silent, even when their own eyes caught betrayal unfolding right before them. They learned that, in Rose Chronicles, secrets were the greatest currency of all — even when that currency was stained with blood.

After all, if these families had gone bankrupt, where was all that money coming from? And why was there never anything left for those still fighting tooth and nail against the revolution Moonlit had once so desperately wanted?

The Source of Wealth

While some families in Rose Chronicles mourned their losses and counted their profits, others celebrated atop the hill with lavish parties and endless drinks. Amid the darkness that filled the homes of those who had perished alongside the industry, the only light came from those who would never have to miss their luxuries.

After all, what they had lost was an industry — the very heart of Ashbourne — not their armored cars or their latest-model phones.

While some families grew even wealthier, carrying on as though nothing had happened, others sought their fortunes elsewhere or attempted to build businesses of their own. Take the Abernathy family, for instance. Despite having almost nothing left after the great economic crisis, they found strength in one another and built a small bakery on 56th Street, in Riverside.

None of the families affected by the crisis understood how those at the top continued to flaunt their wealth, and not once did they dare to question it. What could they possibly do? They were inferior to those who had supposedly brought Ashbourne forward — only to send it tumbling backward almost overnight.

There were rumors that the eldest children of the wealthiest families had married, merging their fortunes and making the already wealthy even richer. Others claimed there had been embezzlement during the industry's closure.

But the truth was right there, before their very eyes, in the calluses on their fingers and among their lost dreams. Rose Chronicles was not recovering, and every promise of economic restoration had become a distant, almost unreachable dream.

After all, for those who are always hungry, not even tens of millions could ever fill their bellies.

The Moonlit Conflict

Moonlit Chronicles was the district that turned its back on whatever the hell was happening in Rose Chronicles. The only things they kept from there were the same county and, consequently, the same surnames.

But Moonlit wasn't like those petty little rich bastards. Not even close.

They didn't need money to lie; they could do it for free. And even then, they pitied those who still suffered beneath the lies hidden behind golden masks that probably cost more than the construction of the Abernathy family's house. And God forgive them for thinking this way, but the only families who were actually making a profit after Ashbourne's downfall were those with enough guts to build a business of their own and get on with their lives.

As though nothing had ever happened there.

Moonlit was furious when those people from Rose Chronicles tried to poison them too, claiming it was God's will and that, if they didn't want to burn in hell, they would follow His commands like puppets. After all, those who still believed in economic restoration were eating out of the rich people's hands like dogs.

They protected their properties, cleaned their clothes, yet were left to face the consequences when their children fell ill — the people of Moonlit would never put up with that kind of shit.

And that was why Ashbourne was divided. Because some still believed in tradition and divine promises, while others had finally recognized the lies plastered across every bloated belly and every fake smile.

The people of Moonlit weren't equally wealthy, either. They still had to endure the heat on sunny days and the merciless cold of snowy winters. But at least they didn't have to live with the constant feeling of being watched, afraid that one of those bastards might blame them for the downfall of the powerful families.

They could live in peace, finish a shift at work, and enjoy a cold beer at the Fletcher family's bar.

And may God forgive them once again, but if that was the price the people of Rose had to pay for Moonlit to keep running without some new shit hitting the fan, then perhaps it was better to let those fools keep believing in the divine promises of the Great Families, all disguised as good Samaritans.
