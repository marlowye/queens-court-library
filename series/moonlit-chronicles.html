THE LIES THAT HOLD US TOGETHER

The Secrets

Prologue

Before the lies, there was a small town nestled in the south of Scotland, known as Ashbourne. A town that, no matter how hard you searched the maps, you would never find. You would probably assume it had been invented by someone who had never set foot there.

But Ashbourne existed. And it still does. Following a territorial dispute, the town was divided in two. Two pieces of a puzzle whose story is far from over.

"A coin has two faces, yet both are one and the same"

History

Before the division, Ashbourne was just another town in the south of Scotland, whose economy depended on a local industry controlled by influential families. For generations, these families had accumulated land, property, and political power. The town grew around this structure, and a great number of its inhabitants depended, directly or indirectly, on that industry to survive.

Until the company fell into crisis and shut its doors.

Hundreds of people lost their jobs. Small businesses disappeared, families lost their homes, and the inequality that had once been concealed beneath an illusion of stability became impossible to ignore.

That was when the dispute began.

The traditional families wanted to preserve their properties and influence. Others advocated for land redistribution, new investments, and a different administration. The disagreement escalated into a territorial and political dispute. After months of conflict, Ashbourne was officially divided into two districts.

But the division solved nothing.

Moonlit's Background

The Moonlit Chronicles had to endure a fierce dispute over land, after all, they were the ones who turned their backs on tradition, the Church, and the words of the wealthy — people they never believed in to begin with. In their search for a better future, the residents of the second district had only one thing in mind: helping one another.

It did not matter if some of them disapproved of certain things in Moonlit as well; they would still embrace each other and say that life there — however dull and scarce — was better than living imprisoned in the cages their masters had built. In Moonlit, they could be whoever they wanted, as long as they did not turn out to be idiots like the people of Rose Chronicles.

The families came together, forming a true community built under pressure and around a shared problem: a lack of money. Don't get me wrong, the people of the first district had money, even if there wasn't much of it, or even if it was dirty. But they had it. They didn't have to rely on the mercy of a god the second district didn't even believe existed.

After all, if such a god existed, where was he when the crisis began? Where was he when rumors about the Ashcroft children began spreading everywhere, eventually crossing the fences into the second district? Everyone knew about the Ashcrofts and the infidelity of one of the industry's partners; but, just as it happened, they preferred to forget the man who betrayed his wife and remember only the young woman who gave birth to two bastard children.

That did not happen in Moonlit, partly because none of the wealthy lived in that region. After all, those who did not believe in traditional family values had no family of their own — and if they did, they still had enough balls and big enough dicks to bury only in their wives and no one else.

That was Moonlit: defying the rules, standing against the Church and its teachings, giving the middle finger to the filthy rich bastards, drinking beer after a day's work, and living with dirty streets; yet still finding happiness amidst the chaos and social ruin.

To hell with it. This was never meant to be a pretty story anyway, because even in Moonlit, there are secrets swept under the rug — the difference is that nobody cares enough to look.

The Cost of Freedom

The economic crisis fucked everyone over, but the residents of Moonlit were undoubtedly the ones hit the hardest. Watching countless residents of Ashbourne sink into the mud their little town had become, they had no other choice but to abandon every dream and promise of a better future.

Fathers began worrying about household bills, mothers about what their children would have to eat the following day, while the children simply wanted to get out of there as soon as possible. They did not deserve this. Truthfully, nobody did. They abandoned their dreams and aspirations just to try and build what Moonlit needed: places for hygiene and survival, education, clothing, and security. The rest could come later, provided they lived long enough to tell the tale.

While they struggled to find the courage to carry on, there was one man who harbored a deep hatred for the wealthy. A man who had been just as deeply affected, having worked in the industry before it shut down — that piece of shit had put food on his family's dinner table, and when it went bankrupt, his employers couldn't even look him in the eye. A bunch of fucking cowards.

Donald McAllister was the first to demand something: perseverance. He was among the first to decide that his two hands were worth more than simply pleasing his wife and patting his children's heads; he could make that fucking mess work if he tried. And so, Moonlit's first police station came into existence — a pathetic little place, but spacious enough to hold prisoners for a few days. Before long, it also became a place where his children would learn the entire alphabet, just so they could spell out: fuck the filthy rich.

The other families found their encouragement in Donald. They realized they could use fear to their advantage, and little by little, Moonlit began to come alive with bars, gathering spots, parks, and alleys dark enough to smoke a joint without anyone noticing or interrupting.

But their dreams were never recovered. There was no bringing back that sparkle in their eyes from when everything still worked — not that things didn't work now, but back when everything worked without demanding sacrifices. Many families lost their dreams, their homes, their livelihoods, and even their own loved ones.

The Families Who Stayed

After the McAllister family took the first step, it was time for the other families to show what they were capable of. Husbands joined their wives and even encouraged them to build something they genuinely enjoyed, something that could benefit the community — or simply provide a little entertainment. It didn't matter. And the perfect example of this was Florence McKenna; the woman simply embraced her artistic side and opened a beauty salon for the women of the community.

Her husband helped out at the Wycliffe family mechanic shop, which, after the eldest son left his father injured and in his mother's care, decided it was time to bring home the bread. And the result? The filthy rich brought their luxury cars there, and the boy made a promise to himself that he wouldn't wreck a single one, because his parents needed the money.

Among the other families were the Fletchers, whose father and son owned the most frequented bar in Moonlit; the entire Callaghan family joined forces and fixed up an old property until it became a little grocery store; and the only son of the Crowleys embraced his pagan side and opened an antique shop in Moonlit's darkest streets. He swore it wasn't themed — it was simply because the streetlights barely worked.

Several families made do with whatever life offered them. Some moved to the big city, while others stayed behind, only taking the road whenever they needed to go there. Everyone found their own way of settling accounts with the devil, and speaking of him, I do hope he embraces these families who needed a little affection and protection so very badly.

Crowley, please have a word with him and convince him.

The God Who Never Came

The people of Moonlit were not against Christianity, of course they weren't! They believed in God and perhaps even more so than the people of the first district, after all, their pastor was always telling lies. How could they possibly trust a man like that?

However, it is fair to say that the younger generation has a certain morbid curiosity about whether God exists or not and, if He does, why He never came to the aid of either district. The Crowley family's son was the first to turn his back on the Church. His parents, who were devoted Christians, could do nothing to stop him; after all, he was already a grown man and could deal with whatever the hell came his way.

Men and women did lose their faith, yes, but there were those who, amidst the storm, held their crucifixes against their chests and begged God to show them the way. Perhaps their prayers were answered, perhaps they weren't... It's difficult to say. I never asked.

But if there is one thing that is absolutely certain about Moonlit, it is this: you may follow any religion you please, as long as you do not take your god's name in vain. Whether that god is Lucifer himself or Jesus Christ.

After all, intolerance and dishonesty were traits of the wealthy in Rose Chronicles, and the people of Moonlit were poor. How could they possibly be guilty of such things?
